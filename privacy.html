Privacy

Last updated 6 November 2026 · For IT teams, see Security

The short version

Your calendar is read and analysed in your browser. It is never uploaded to us, and we never see it.

Calendar files

When you choose an .ics file, your browser reads it on your device. Every number, chart and image is worked out there. Nothing from the file is sent to our servers. A summary is kept in your browser so the page survives a refresh. It includes names and meeting titles, so it is cleared automatically after 30 days. If you switch off Keep results on this device, it lives only in that tab and is gone when you close it. “Start over” deletes it straight away.

Want to be sure? Open the page, switch off your Wi-Fi, then add your calendar. Everything still works, because nothing is sent.

Things you add yourself

Time off, trips and moments you add on the “Anything your calendar missed?” step are kept in your browser on this device, under the same rules as your results: 30 days, or only this tab in private mode. They are never sent anywhere. “Start over” removes them.

Google Calendar

If you choose Connect Google Calendar, Google asks you to grant read-only access to your calendar events. Your browser then fetches your events directly from Google and analyses them on your device, exactly as with a file. The access token stays in memory and is revoked as soon as your events have loaded. We never receive the token or your events.

Our use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Calendar data is used only to show you your own summary, is not stored by us, is not transferred to anyone, and is not used for advertising or to train models.

Outlook

If you choose Connect Outlook, Microsoft asks you to grant read-only access to your calendar. Your browser fetches your events directly from Microsoft and analyses them on your device. The sign-in token stays in memory and is discarded once your events have loaded. We never receive the token or your events.

Shared links, images and videos

A shared link contains only the numbers shown on your card, packed into the address itself. When someone opens it, our server reads those numbers to draw the preview image that chat apps and social sites show, and stores nothing. We don’t keep request logs. Names and meeting titles are left out unless you switch them on. Images and videos you save are created on your device and never uploaded.

Unlocking by sharing

Until you unlock, your share links also carry a random invite code, such as ?r=k3x9q2m8p1z4. It is made in your browser and isn't linked to your name, email or calendar. When someone opens your link and then makes their own Wrapped from a real calendar, their browser sends us your invite code and their own random code. We store a count against your code and a one-way scrambled version of theirs, so each person counts once. Both are deleted after about a year. Nothing about either calendar is sent.

Team reports

Team reports are built in your browser from files you add. Please ask your colleagues before using their calendars. Nothing is uploaded.

If you ask us to email your plan

This is the only time anything derived from your calendar reaches us. We send the plan text to the address you give, using our email provider, and we do not keep the plan. If you tick the check-in box, we keep your email address so we can send a short check-in each quarter. Every email has a one-click link that deletes it, and you can ask us to delete it at any time.

Payments

Payments are handled by Stripe. We receive confirmation that a payment succeeded, not your card details.

Analytics

We count a handful of anonymous events, such as “story finished” or “card saved”, as daily totals. There are no cookies, no identifiers, no IP addresses and no calendar data in these counts.

If the app hits an error, we receive the type of error and where in our code it happened. Anything quoted, and any name, address, email or number, is removed before it leaves your browser, and again on our server.
